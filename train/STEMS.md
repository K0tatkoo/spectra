# Spectra's own stem model: the plan

**Status: milestone 1 (data) done on 2026-11-04. Nothing is trained yet.** Until
this model wins, the Stems page keeps StemgenRT v0.6.1, in free builds only.

## Why

The Stems page runs StemgenRT v0.6.1. On 2026-10-27 its publisher withdrew the
v0.6.x weights pending a provenance and rights review. The publisher also says
the repository's MIT licence does not clear them for commercial use. StemgenRT
v0.7 is CC BY-NC-SA, trained on MUSDB18-HQ and MoisesDB, and no commercial
licence is offered. The other well-known separators have the same problem.
Demucs's weights were reportedly described by their author as research-only,
and the question is still unanswered on their model pages. Open-Unmix's
large model is non-commercial. The rest were trained on the same non-commercial
datasets and carry no licence. The synth lane's own weights are CC BY-NC-SA too,
because they were trained on MoisesDB and MUSDB18-HQ vocals.

So a paid Spectra needs a separation model trained by Spectra, on data whose
licence allows commercial use. The goal is quality as good as today's or better.

**Decided (2026-11-04):**
- Spectra trains its own model, rather than licensing an SDK.
- In file mode, the model separates ahead of the playhead. Nothing is stored.
- Free data sources only: no paid datasets and no recording sessions for now.
- The free builds keep StemgenRT v0.6.1 meanwhile, with its status stated in
  Settings, the README and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.

## The rules (this is what makes it sellable)

1. **Only allow-listed licences.** These are CC0, public domain, and CC BY (any
   version), plus audio generated by Spectra's own code. These are refused:
   - NonCommercial (NC), as in MUSDB18, MoisesDB and MedleyDB.
   - NoDerivatives (ND).
   - ShareAlike (SA), so the weights' licence stays Spectra's to choose.
   - "Research only" terms, and anything with no stated licence.
2. **A provenance ledger.** There is one row per file: source, URL, licence, credit
   line, SHA-256 and the date it was fetched.
   - The data loader opens only files in the ledger.
   - A training run refuses to start if any row's licence is off the list.
   - The CC BY credits in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` are generated from the ledger.

   StemgenRT had no such record, and that is why it was withdrawn.
3. **No other model's output, anywhere.** No StemgenRT stems as inputs, no
   pseudo-labels from Demucs or anyone else, and no teacher initialisation. The
   synth splitter trained on StemgenRT's outputs. This model will not.
4. **No non-commercial data even for scoring.** MUSDB18-HQ and MoisesDB stay out
   of this pipeline entirely. Quality is measured on held-out clean data, and by
   listening to the owner's own songs. Those songs are only listened to, never
   trained on.
5. A separate folder on the desktop (`D:\stems-clean`). The synth runs' MUSDB and
   MoisesDB copies are never read by this pipeline.

## Data (free sources only)

Every licence below was read on the source's own page on 2026-11-04, and each
source module (`ownstems/sources/`) quotes where.

Checked and left out:
- **Refused:** The Spheres (CC BY-SA); every real choir and singing set that
  was checked (Dagstuhl ChoirSet, Choral Singing Dataset, Cantoría, ESMUC:
  CC BY-NC; CSD: CC BY-NC-SA; JVS-MuSiC: research only); IDMT-SMT-Bass and
  -Guitar (CC BY-NC-ND).
- **Later, if needed:** StemGMD (CC BY 4.0, but one 211 GB split archive, more
  than the desktop has free, while E-GMD covers drums); NSynth and the
  Salamander piano samples (allowed, low value for now).
- **Need the owner's own account and terms:** Freesound (originals need OAuth2,
  and each sound carries a generative-AI preference to respect) and Common
  Voice (Mozilla Data Collective).
- **Unclear, so out:** NUS-48E (an anonymous re-upload), SingStyle111, URMP,
  MusicNet, VSCO-2 CE.

A caveat: Slakh2100 and StemGMD are renders of commercial sample libraries. The
datasets are published as CC BY, and that is what we rely on. Whether the
sample vendors' terms reach rendered audio is the publishers' question, and it
is not stated.

**Vocals are the weak spot.** Free singing data is small and mostly dry. To
narrow the gap, every vocal source goes through a made-up production chain:
doubling, delay, reverb, saturation, pitch-snap, formant shifts and EQ.

**Mixing** builds a fresh song for every example:
- Each stem comes from a different source or song, at a random level, sometimes
  silent.
- Pitch and tempo shift a little, and EQ and stereo width are drawn at random.
- A master bus applies compression and limiting down to about −6 LUFS, because
  hardstyle masters are brick-walled. The limiter's gain is computed on the mix
  and applied to every stem alike, so the stems still sum to the mix.

Twelve example mixes (12 s, valid split) are in `listen-m1/` on the Mac: each
folder has the mix and its stems. What the first renders showed, now fixed:
- VocalSet's zip was written by macOS Archive Utility without zip64 records,
  so Python could not read it past 4 GB. `ownstems/zips.py` finds each entry's
  true offset in memory; the file and its checksum are untouched.
- The loudness gain came after the limiter, so no mix was really mastered. The
  bus now runs compressor → makeup gain to the target → limiter at −0.3 dBFS,
  and loud targets come out brick-walled (−7 dBFS RMS in the renders).

Open for milestone 2:
- **Vocals are 86 % speech by length.** Set `MixConfig.source_weights` so
  singing dominates (VocalSet ×4 or so), and add ccMixter once the owner OKs its
  terms. ccMixter's API terms say that using the API means agreeing to them.
- One example takes about a second to build on one core. Training will need
  several loader processes; measure before the first long run.

## The models

Five stems, as the page has now: vocals, synth (leads and pads), other, bass
(synth bass included), drums. They sum to the mix, through a mixture-consistency
projection.

There are two models, from one band-split codebase of Spectra's own. The
HS-TasNet code (Phil Wang, MIT) may be reused for the live model.

- **File model (first).** It sees the music on both sides of each moment, a few
  seconds of context in overlapping chunks. Offline separators are far ahead of
  real-time ones: Demucs-class models score ~9 dB, against StemgenRT's ~4.5–6.
  That gap is where "better" comes from, even with less real data.
  - Runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine through Core ML.
  - Budget: at least 3× real time on the iPhone 17 Pro Max, so it can stay well
    ahead of the playhead without heating the phone.
- **Live model (second).** For mic and other-apps input.
  - Causal, with ~20 ms of lookahead (today: 2.9 ms). The picture can lag the
    sound by that much without anyone noticing; the synth lane already lags
    12–23 ms.
  - 512-sample hops: 86 calls a second instead of 344.
  - Runs on the CPU through ONNX Runtime as now, or on the Neural Engine. On
    the iPhone, Stems sits at load 0.38 on one core today.
  - Android gets it through ONNX. The S24 is heat-capped, so it may need a
    smaller variant.

## Ahead of the playhead (iOS file mode)

- A second reader opens the same file and decodes from the playhead onward. It
  resamples to 44.1 kHz and runs the file model chunk by chunk on a background
  queue. It writes the five stems into rings indexed by file frame.
- The scopes read the stems at the player's own clock: the segment-queue clock
  `AVAudioPlayerNode` already needs.
- When play starts or after a seek, the lanes stay empty until the first chunk
  is done (~1–2 s, to be measured). A seek drops everything and restarts at the
  new position. During a pause, it works on until it is some seconds ahead, then
  idles. Nothing is written to disk.
- Mic and other-apps input keep the live path.

## Milestones

1. **Data.** Fetchers, ledger, licence check, the mixer, and counts in hours per
   stem. The code is written on the Mac; downloads and preparation run on the
   Windows desktop (which has to be on and on the tailnet).
   `tools/stems_data.py` runs it: `fetch`, `index`, `check`, `report`,
   `credits`, `render` (example mixes to listen to). The code is `ownstems/`,
   and its tests are `tests/test_ownstems.py`.
2. **File model v0.** A few nights on the desktop GPU (RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB).
   - Held-out clean scores per stem.
   - Renders of the owner's hardstyle songs next to StemgenRT's, judged by ear,
     lane by lane.
3. **iOS file mode.** Core ML conversion, the ahead-of-playhead reader, and
   speed / heat / battery measured on the iPhone.
4. **Live model.** The same judgement against StemgenRT, then Android.
5. **The swap.** When the owner prefers ours by ear:
   - Remove StemgenRT and the CC BY-NC-SA synth splitter.
   - Generate the notices from the ledger.
   - Release.

## Risks, said plainly

- **Synthetic data often does not carry over to real songs.** The synth lane
  showed it: +3 dB on made-up songs, false alarms on real ones. The fixes are
  variety, a realistic master bus and real CC-licensed material where it exists.
  The proof is listening to real songs, not held-out scores.
- **Free vocal data is thin.** The live vocals lane may start weaker than
  StemgenRT's. If it stays weaker after the production chain, the levers are
  recording sessions or a paid licence. Both were set aside for now; neither is
  ruled out.
- **The file model's speed on the Neural Engine is a guess** until it is measured
  on the phone.
