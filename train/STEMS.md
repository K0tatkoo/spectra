# Spectra's own stem model: the plan

**Status: milestone 1 (data) done on 2026-11-04. Milestone 2 (the file model):
v0 trained 2026-11-17 → 24 (held-out mean 4.87 dB); listening test next.** Until
this model wins, the Stems page keeps StemgenRT v0.6.1, in free builds only.

## Why

The Stems page runs StemgenRT v0.6.1. On 2026-10-27 its publisher withdrew the
v0.6.x weights pending a provenance and rights review. The publisher also says
the repository's MIT licence does not clear them for commercial use. StemgenRT
v0.7 is CC BY-NC-SA, trained on MUSDB18-HQ and MoisesDB, and no commercial
licence is offered. The other well-known separators have the same problem.
Demucs's weights were reportedly described by their author as research-only,
and the question is still unanswered on their model pages. Open-Unmix's
large model is non-commercial. The rest were trained on the same non-commercial
datasets and carry no licence. The synth lane's own weights are CC BY-NC-SA too,
because they were trained on MoisesDB and MUSDB18-HQ vocals.

So a paid Spectra needs a separation model trained by Spectra, on data whose
licence allows commercial use. The goal is quality as good as today's or better.

**Decided (2026-11-04):**
- Spectra trains its own model, rather than licensing an SDK.
- In file mode, the model separates ahead of the playhead. Nothing is stored.
- Free data sources only: no paid datasets and no recording sessions for now.
- The free builds keep StemgenRT v0.6.1 meanwhile, with its status stated in
  Settings, the README and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.

## The rules (this is what makes it sellable)

1. **Only allow-listed licences.** These are CC0, public domain, and CC BY (any
   version), plus audio generated by Spectra's own code. These are refused:
   - NonCommercial (NC), as in MUSDB18, MoisesDB and MedleyDB.
   - NoDerivatives (ND).
   - ShareAlike (SA), so the weights' licence stays Spectra's to choose.
   - "Research only" terms, and anything with no stated licence.
2. **A provenance ledger.** There is one row per file: source, URL, licence, credit
   line, SHA-256 and the date it was fetched.
   - The data loader opens only files in the ledger.
   - A training run refuses to start if any row's licence is off the list.
   - The CC BY credits in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` are generated from the ledger.

   StemgenRT had no such record, and that is why it was withdrawn.
3. **No other model's output, anywhere.** No StemgenRT stems as inputs, no
   pseudo-labels from Demucs or anyone else, and no teacher initialisation. The
   synth splitter trained on StemgenRT's outputs. This model will not.
4. **No non-commercial data even for scoring.** MUSDB18-HQ and MoisesDB stay out
   of this pipeline entirely. Quality is measured on held-out clean data, and by
   listening to the owner's own songs. Those songs are only listened to, never
   trained on.
5. A separate folder on the desktop (`D:\stems-clean`). The synth runs' MUSDB and
   MoisesDB copies are never read by this pipeline.

Checked and left out:
- **Refused:** The Spheres (CC BY-SA); every real choir and singing set that
  was checked (Dagstuhl ChoirSet, Choral Singing Dataset, Cantoría, ESMUC:
  CC BY-NC; CSD: CC BY-NC-SA; JVS-MuSiC: research only); IDMT-SMT-Bass and
  -Guitar (CC BY-NC-ND).
- **Later, if needed:** StemGMD (CC BY 4.0, but one 211 GB split archive, more
  than the desktop has free, while E-GMD covers drums); NSynth and the
  Salamander piano samples (allowed, low value for now).
- **Need the owner's own account and terms:** Freesound (originals need OAuth2,
  and each sound carries a generative-AI preference to respect) and Common
  Voice (Mozilla Data Collective).
- **Unclear, so out:** NUS-48E (an anonymous re-upload), SingStyle111, URMP,
  MusicNet, VSCO-2 CE.

A caveat: Slakh2100 and StemGMD are renders of commercial sample libraries. The
datasets are published as CC BY, and that is what we rely on. Whether the
sample vendors' terms reach rendered audio is the publishers' question, and it
is not stated.

**Vocals are the weak spot.** Free singing data is small and mostly dry. To
narrow the gap, every vocal source goes through a made-up production chain:
EQ, then doubling, saturation, delay and reverb in some mix. Pitch-snap and
formant shifts are not written yet.

**Mixing** builds a fresh song for every example (`ownstems/mixer.py`):
- Remixes (three in four): each stem comes from a different source or song and
  is sometimes left out. Stems are levelled by perceived loudness (LUFS, as
  BS.1770 measures it), not RMS, each in its own random range; the accompaniment
  ("other") sits a few dB under the vocals and drums, as in real songs. EQ,
  stereo width and polarity are drawn at random. No pitch or tempo shifts yet.
- Generated hardstyle songs (one in four): drums, reverse bass and synths from
  one made-up song, 145–158 BPM in a minor key, the synths ducked by the kick.
  Their parts are never mixed with catalog drums, bass or synth. A vocal joins
  half of them, and an "other" part rarely and low.
- A master bus: compressor, makeup gain to a target loudness, and a limiter at
  −0.3 dBFS, so loud targets come out brick-walled as hardstyle masters are
  (−5.6 to −9.6 LUFS in the generated songs). The gain is computed on the mix and
  applied to every stem alike, so the stems still sum to the mix.

Listening sets on the Mac, each folder holding a mix and its stems: `listen-m1/`

- VocalSet's zip was written by macOS Archive Utility without zip64 records,
  so Python could not read it past 4 GB. `ownstems/zips.py` finds each entry's
  true offset in memory; the file and its checksum are untouched.
- The loudness gain came after the limiter, so no mix was really mastered. The
  bus now runs compressor → makeup gain to the target → limiter at −0.3 dBFS,
  and loud targets come out brick-walled (−7 dBFS RMS in the renders).
- The owner listened (2026-11-05): the "generated songs" were not hardstyle, and
  the other stem stood out. Both were real. The generated songs used the generic
  tempo range (70–180 BPM) and borrowed catalog drums and bass from other songs;
  and RMS levelling left bright parts up to ~12 dB louder than deep ones. Over
  150 remixes "other" is now the loudest stem 12 % of the times it plays, with a
  median 7.8 LU under the mix (vocals: 3.9).
- The owner then heard the generated drums as 808s, and they were: the kick
  drove a deep sine once, leaving 68-78 % of the drums' energy under 120 Hz
  and the hats buried. The kick is now drive → thinned fundamental → mid boost
  → drive, with a clean sub under it (drums: 23-37 % under 120 Hz, 23-38 % in
  the 250 Hz-1 kHz "tok" band), and the kit has louder hats and claps, open hats,
  and a crash per 8-bar phrase. `listen-hardstyle/kick-before-after.wav` plays
  8 old kicks, then 8 new, euphoric and then raw.

Open for milestone 2:
- **Vocals are 86 % speech by length.** Done in milestone 2: ccMixter added
  (owner OK 2026-11-17) and the draw weights favour singing.
- One example takes about a second to build on one core. Training will need
  several loader processes; measure before the first long run.

## Milestone 2: the file model, training on the home server (started 2026-11-16)

The owner asked for the training to run on the server instead of the desktop,
at about half load (full load is loud), for as many days as it takes. The
server has no GPU: two Xeon E5-2630L (12 cores, 24 threads, 2.0 GHz, AVX but
no AVX2), 62 GB RAM.

**Data.** The desktop was off, so the server fetched every source again from
its original host into `~/stems-clean` (same layout, same ledger, each archive
checked against its published MD5; `fetch-all.sh` there, log in `logs/`).
The owner OK'd ccMixter's API terms on 2026-11-17 and it was fetched that day:
of 1,507 CC BY / CC0 uploads tagged a cappella, 328 kept (the rest had no file
labelled as a vocal, or were tagged non-commercial, AI-generated or remix),
522 files, 25.7 h of real produced vocals (24.3 train / 1.4 held out), all CC
BY 2.5/3.0 or CC0. Its file server answers 403 without a Referer (hotlink
protection), so downloads send the upload's own page; a few old records are
Latin-1 inside UTF-8 pages.

**Model** (`ownstems/model.py`): a band-split transformer after BS-RoFormer,
written here. STFT 2048 / hop 512; mel-spaced bands (3 bins = 64 Hz wide in the
bass); per band a linear layer to one vector; blocks of attention along time
(both sides of each moment) and then across bands, with rotary positions; per
band a head giving a complex mask for each stem, channel and bin; mixture
consistency, so the five stems sum to the mix exactly. The mix is normalised by
its own level going in. Presets: `tiny` 4.5 M parameters, `small` 6.4 M (the
first run), `base`. Most parameters sit in the per-band heads; the transformer
core is small.

**Training** (`train_stems.py`): fresh made-up songs every step from the mixer
in worker processes, 4 s examples, batch 4; loss = L1 on the waveforms + L1 on
STFT magnitudes at three resolutions, on level-normalised audio; AdamW, warm-up
then cosine; averaged weights (EMA) are what is validated and kept. Vocals are
drawn ~45 % from ccMixter (×10), ~28 % from VocalSet (×6), ~27 % from
LibriTTS-R's speech (until step ~2,500: VocalSet ×6 and speech, half each).
Validation: 32 held-out songs (split `valid`, built once, `valid.pt`): per
stem the SDR where it plays, next to "the whole mix is this stem" (the number
to beat), and the SDR over the generated hardstyle songs. `valid.pt` records
what it was drawn from (clips per source, draw weights). When that changes (a
new source, new weights), the next start keeps the old set and its best weights
as `valid-until-<step>.pt` / `best-until-<step>.pt`, builds a new set, scores
it at once, and "best" starts over on it.
`tools/stems-train.sh start|status|pause|resume|stop` runs it for days:
pinned CPUs, nice 19, idle I/O, restart from the last checkpoint after a crash,
`@reboot` in the user's crontab. `tools/separate.py` renders real songs into
stems with a checkpoint, for listening.

**Speed on the server** (batch 4 × 4 s, 2026-11-16): `small` 11.7 s a step on
12 cores (one thread each), `tiny` 7.4 s; on 6 cores × 2 hyperthreads, `tiny`
10.4 s. Attention takes ~25 % (CPU flash attention is already the fastest
path there), matrix products ~20 %, the rest is many small element-wise ops.
**Half load, measured** (5 min of real training each; ARK was awake and the
Slakh download running, so between runs the server sat at 140 W and fans at
~5,000-5,800 RPM; truly idle it is 98 W and ~2,900 RPM):

808 and no real drums; bass is silent; synth is fair but drops out for no
reason; vocals let through far more background than today; other lets less
through. Measured over the 15 songs, share of each band's energy per lane
(app / v0): sub < 120 Hz to drums 18 / 88 %, to bass 82 / 9 %; > 6 kHz (hats)
to drums 90 / 3 %, to vocals 4 / 50 %, to synth 6 / 47 %; 2-6 kHz to vocals
12 / 64 %. Cause: our generated hardstyle songs label the whole kick, pitched
tail included, as drums (only the offbeat reverse bass is bass, in half the
songs), and their hats and claps are plain filtered-noise hits, so real
layered hardstyle percussion was never taught as drums.

**The desktop GPU, measured (RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB, fp32 + TF32):** `small` batch
4 × 4 s 0.32 s a step (49× the server), 4.8 GB; batch 8 × 4 s 0.66 s, 9.6 GB;
`base` 4 × 4 s 0.62 s, 8.8 GB; `base` 6 × 6 s spills past 16 GB (52 s a
step); a ~25 M model does not fit without bf16 / activation checkpointing.
Memory, not speed, is the limit (~0.3 GB per second of audio in a batch for
`small`). Building one 6 s example takes 0.28 s on one Ryzen core, so a few
loader workers keep up.

For scale: the desktop's RTX 4060 Ti would be very roughly 50–100× faster, so
a week on the server is a first model that shows what the data can do, not
the finished one. The same script runs on CUDA unchanged when the desktop is
free.

**A bug the real data found:** about one batch in ten had a NaN gradient at
the very start. A generated synth whose filter or reverb tail decays into
denormal floats: `abs()` of a complex STFT bin has the gradient z · (1/|z|),
the reciprocal overflows, and 0 · inf = NaN. The magnitude is now
√(re² + im² + 1e-12), denormals are flushed (also faster on x86), and any
non-finite step is skipped and its batch saved as `nonfinite-<step>.pt`.

## The models

Five stems, as the page has now: vocals, synth (leads and pads), other, bass
(synth bass included), drums. They sum to the mix, through a mixture-consistency
projection.

There are two models, from one band-split codebase of Spectra's own. The
HS-TasNet code (Phil Wang, MIT) may be reused for the live model.

- **File model (first).** It sees the music on both sides of each moment, a few
  seconds of context in overlapping chunks. Offline separators are far ahead of
  real-time ones: Demucs-class models score ~9 dB, against StemgenRT's ~4.5–6.
  That gap is where "better" comes from, even with less real data.
  - Runs on the iPhone's Neural Engine through Core ML.
  - Budget: at least 3× real time on the iPhone 17 Pro Max, so it can stay well
    ahead of the playhead without heating the phone.
- **Live model (second).** For mic and other-apps input.
  - Causal, with ~20 ms of lookahead (today: 2.9 ms). The picture can lag the
    sound by that much without anyone noticing; the synth lane already lags
    12–23 ms.
  - 512-sample hops: 86 calls a second instead of 344.
  - Runs on the CPU through ONNX Runtime as now, or on the Neural Engine. On
    the iPhone, Stems sits at load 0.38 on one core today.
  - Android gets it through ONNX. The S24 is heat-capped, so it may need a
    smaller variant.

## Ahead of the playhead (iOS file mode)

- A second reader opens the same file and decodes from the playhead onward. It
  resamples to 44.1 kHz and runs the file model chunk by chunk on a background
  queue. It writes the five stems into rings indexed by file frame.
- The scopes read the stems at the player's own clock: the segment-queue clock
  `AVAudioPlayerNode` already needs.
- When play starts or after a seek, the lanes stay empty until the first chunk
  is done (~1–2 s, to be measured). A seek drops everything and restarts at the
  new position. During a pause, it works on until it is some seconds ahead, then
  idles. Nothing is written to disk.
- Mic and other-apps input keep the live path.

1. **Data.** Fetchers, ledger, licence check, the mixer, and counts in hours per
   stem. The code is written on the Mac; downloads and preparation run on the

   `tools/stems_data.py` runs it: `fetch`, `index`, `check`, `report`,

   - Held-out clean scores per stem.
   - Renders of the owner's hardstyle songs next to StemgenRT's, judged by ear,
     lane by lane.
3. **iOS file mode.** Core ML conversion, the ahead-of-playhead reader, and
   speed / heat / battery measured on the iPhone.
4. **Live model.** The same judgement against StemgenRT, then Android.
5. **The swap.** When the owner prefers ours by ear:
   - Remove StemgenRT and the CC BY-NC-SA synth splitter.
   - Generate the notices from the ledger.
   - Release.

## Risks, said plainly

- **Synthetic data often does not carry over to real songs.** The synth lane
  showed it: +3 dB on made-up songs, false alarms on real ones. The fixes are
  variety, a realistic master bus and real CC-licensed material where it exists.
  The proof is listening to real songs, not held-out scores.
- **Free vocal data is thin.** The live vocals lane may start weaker than
  StemgenRT's. If it stays weaker after the production chain, the levers are
  recording sessions or a paid licence. Both were set aside for now; neither is
  ruled out.
- **The file model's speed on the Neural Engine is a guess** until it is measured
  on the phone.
